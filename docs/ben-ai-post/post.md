## Title

Sell AI or build AI? There's room for both.

## Post

Hi, my name is Josh, and I'm a recovering operator.

It has been 0 days since I tried to close a sales call and fix an automation in the same afternoon.

Here is how the two lanes explain the exact same product.

**The builder:** "So the agent pulls the document, classifies it, extracts the fields, routes the exceptions to a queue, and then, wait, I skipped step 3."

**The seller:** "It reads your paperwork so your people don't have to."

Same product. One of them just booked a meeting.

I know the builder's voice well, because it's mine. Business partners have told me that when I'm ready to go, I'm on step 10 while they're still figuring out step 1. They were right.

**The serious part (short, I promise)**

Selling AI and building AI are two different jobs. Different focus, different kind of thinking, and doing both at once is how you get mediocre at both and tired at everything. I spent years on the front lines of sales while also building the back end of the automations in the same business. It works about as well as juggling chainsaws. Technically possible.

Builders make the thing. We are the worst at explaining what it does for a human being.

Relationship people translate it into plain language, earn trust, and bring in the conversations that drive revenue.

Neither one is the hero. A product with no relationships is a science project. Relationships with no product are a very nice lunch.

So I partnered with someone who has a sales background and a track record. My lane is building. His lane is relationships. We both stay in our lane, and neither of us pretends to be the other.

**Now the part I care about most: the people**

Echo 1 Labs is Business Lifecycle Management. Agents run the work, your people approve it, and you see all of it in Agent Orbit. But a stack is only as good as the relationships standing in front of it, so here is who I'd like to meet:

**1. Relationship builders who want to white label or resell.**
You own the customer relationship. You are the name they trust. We build and support behind you, and a real person is always reachable when your customer calls. I will run the first demo with you as your sales engineer, then we rehearse, and you run the second one.

**2. Builders who want to build with us on a contract basis.**
Bring your craft. Skip the part where you explain it to a stranger on a Tuesday.

What I ask of both: be trustworthy, hold the line, stay mission-driven. I would rather have three people who show up than thirty who ghost.
